01 // THE BEHAVIORAL CRISIS

Death by ₹30 UPI taps. Why college students go broke without buying anything big.

In behavioral economics (Soman, 2001), payment decoupling describes how cashless instant transactions remove the physical friction of money leaving your hand. When you pay ₹50 for tea or ₹120 for canteen lunch via phone screen, your brain logs zero emotional resistance.

You never spent ₹5,000 in one go. You made 6 micro-taps a day for 15 days. Corporate adult budgeting apps fail students because they ask for 12 accounting fields per record. By day 7, you stop logging. By day 18, you are borrowing from flatmates.

"Passive spreadsheet bookkeeping fails to alter consumer habits without active prescriptive guidance. What students need isn't historical graphs; they need to know what they can safely spend before 11:59 PM today."

Relational Database Workbench (3NF)

Third Normal Form

Normalized schema dictionary with foreign key constraints across 6 relational tables:

users categories transactions budgets savings_goals recurring_expenses
SELECT c.name, COUNT(t.id) AS tx_count, SUM(t.amount) AS total_spent
FROM transactions t
JOIN categories c ON t.category_id = c.id
WHERE t.is_need = 1
GROUP BY c.name ORDER BY total_spent DESC;
